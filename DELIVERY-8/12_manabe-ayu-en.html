Do you know the fish called ayu?

In Japan, there is a fish written with the characters for "fragrant fish" and called ayu (sweetfish). True to its name, this river fish carries a remarkably fresh, clean aroma reminiscent of cucumber or watermelon — a delicate, almost floral fragrance that seems impossible for a fish.

Ayu live for just one year. Born in spring, they swim upstream through clear currents in summer, completing a single turn of the seasons. That is why ayu are also called "year fish" . They spend their entire lives in only the clearest of rivers.

Ayu can survive only in pure water. Crystal-clear streams are their one and only home. For their beauty and noble spirit, ayu have long been celebrated as the "Queen of Clear Streams."





One family has been simmering this queen for generations.

In Miyoshi City, Tokushima. Manabe Honke has been carrying on the tradition of their signature "ayu no sugata-ni" (whole simmered sweetfish) for generations, continuing to craft it over many long years.

Manabe Honke's primary trade is, in fact, a soy sauce and miso brewery. They are fermentation artisans who brew their own soy sauce brand, "Manazuru Shoyu." Using their own soy sauce alongside premium Awa Bancha (a regional tea), these craftspeople slowly simmer whole ayu in the traditional kanro-ni (sweet soy glaze) style, taking their time to coax out every layer of flavor.

And every single fish is prepared entirely by hand — one by one. The depth of flavor that only a soy sauce maker could achieve. The craft of Japan, the land of fermentation, lives within each fish. That is the sugata-ni of Manabe Honke.





A single fish, a feast nurtured by clear streams.

Manabe Honke's egg-bearing ayu simmered whole is a masterpiece — an entire sweetfish prepared as kanro-ni. Simmered until even the bones are tender, every part of the fish can be savored from head to tail, leaving nothing behind. With a single bite, the delicate pop of the roe held within and a deep, umami-rich flavor spread across the palate.

Water that flows endlessly — cutting through mountains, washing over rocks, gathering from natural springs. Within this single fish lives the bounty nurtured by Japan's rural clear streams, and the soy sauce craft that Manabe Honke has inherited and preserved.

Carefully prepared one by one, this is a product made in limited quantities. The gifts of Japan's clear streams, delivered straight to your table — approximately 17 cm, each fish individually and carefully wrapped.
